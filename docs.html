OnTrace Documentation

Everything you need to use OnTrace — the free Solana toolbox to scan, clean, swap, send, create and trace.

What OnTrace is. A website that works with your Solana wallet. It reads public blockchain data to show what's in a wallet and how risky each token is, and it builds transactions (cleanup, swaps, sends, token creation) that you approve in your own wallet.

What it isn't. OnTrace is not a wallet, exchange or custodian. It never holds your funds, never sees your seed phrase, and has no server that stores your data.

Requirements

  • A Solana wallet — Phantom, Solflare or Backpack. Only needed for actions; read-only tools work without one.
  • A little SOL for network fees (each transaction costs a fraction of a cent). Token creation and multi-send also need SOL for account deposits.
  • A modern browser — Chrome, Brave, Edge, Firefox or Safari, or your wallet app's built-in browser on a phone.

Connecting a wallet

  1. Open ontrace.space. Wait until the top-right button changes from Loading… to Connect wallet.
  2. Click Connect wallet. If you have more than one wallet installed, choose which one.
  3. Approve the connection in your wallet. Connecting only shares your public address — it does not let OnTrace move anything.
  4. The button now shows your wallet name and a short version of your address.

To disconnect, click Disconnect next to the wallet button. You can also revoke OnTrace's connection from your wallet's settings (Phantom: Settings → Connected apps).

Tip: If you scanned a wallet before connecting, OnTrace re-scans automatically once you connect so the cleanup buttons unlock.

Using a phone

Phone wallets only connect to websites opened inside the wallet app's own browser.

  1. Open ontrace.space in your normal phone browser and tap Connect wallet.
  2. Tap Open in Phantom or Open in Solflare. Your wallet app opens with OnTrace loaded.
  3. Tap Connect wallet again inside the wallet app and approve.

Using another wallet? Open its built-in browser (Phantom: the compass icon; Solflare: Explore; Backpack: Browser) and type ontrace.space.

Mainnet & devnet

The network menu (top right) switches between:

  • Mainnet — the real Solana network with real money. Use this normally.
  • Devnet — Solana's test network with free test SOL. Use it to practise token creation, multi-send and cleanup without risk. Your wallet must also be switched to devnet (Phantom: Settings → Developer settings → Testnet mode). Free test SOL: faucet.solana.com.

Swaps work on mainnet only, because Jupiter routes real markets.

Solana basics (in plain English)

Token account
Your wallet keeps each token in its own small "account". Every token account holds a refundable deposit of about 0.002 SOL (called rent).
Empty token account
When you sell or send all of a token, its account usually stays open — with the 0.002 SOL still locked inside. Closing it returns the SOL. Active traders can have hundreds of these.
Token Program & Token-2022
The two systems Solana uses for tokens. Token-2022 adds extras like on-chain names, transfer fees and transfer hooks. OnTrace supports both.
Mint authority
Permission to create more of a token. If it's still active, the creator can increase supply (and dilute holders). Locked/revoked means supply is fixed.
Freeze authority
Permission to freeze a holder's tokens so they can't move or sell them. Common in scams; also used by some regulated stablecoins.
Approval (delegate)
Permission you gave someone to move a certain amount of a token from your account. Old or unknown approvals are a risk.
Permanent delegate
A Token-2022 setting that lets one address move tokens out of any holder's wallet at any time. Extremely risky.
Wrapped SOL (wSOL)
SOL packaged as a token so some apps can use it. Unwrapping turns it back into normal SOL.
Liquidity
Money in a token's trading pool. Low liquidity means big price swings and difficulty selling.
Market cap
Token price × supply. On its own it can be misleading — a token with a $50M market cap but $500 of liquidity is effectively worthless.
Slippage
How much worse than the quote you'll accept if the price moves before your swap lands.
Price impact
How much your own trade moves the price. High impact means the pool is small for your trade size.
Program / pool address
An address controlled by code, not a person (technically "off-curve"). OnTrace labels these so you can tell people from pools.

Portfolio & cleanup

Your wallet's dashboard: what you hold, what's risky, and what you can clean up.

Scan a wallet

  1. Open the Portfolio & cleanup tab.
  2. Your own wallet: connect, leave the address box empty, and press Scan.
    Any other wallet: paste its address and press Scan (read-only — cleanup buttons stay locked).

The scan shows:

CardMeaning
Portfolio valueSOL plus tokens at current prices (tokens rated Likely scam are excluded)
Wallet health0–100 score — see Health score
SOL to reclaimTotal deposits locked in empty token accounts
Risky tokensTokens rated High risk or Likely scam
Active approvalsToken accounts where someone else can move your tokens

Reclaim SOL

  1. After scanning your connected wallet, check the line under Reclaim locked SOL — it shows how many empty accounts and how much SOL you'll get back.
  2. Click Reclaim SOL and approve in your wallet. Up to 20 accounts are closed per transaction; large wallets need several approvals.
  3. OnTrace confirms each transaction, shows links, and re-scans.
What you sign: "Close account" instructions on token accounts that hold zero tokens. Accounts with any balance are never included.

Burn junk tokens

  1. In Your tokens, tick tokens you want gone. High-risk tokens worth under $1 are pre-ticked; untick anything you want to keep.
  2. The line at the bottom shows how many are selected, the SOL you'll get back, and the estimated value being destroyed.
  3. Click Burn selected & reclaim, read the confirmation, and approve in your wallet (up to 9 tokens per transaction).
Burning is permanent. Burned tokens can't be recovered. Never burn something you might want later.

Revoke approvals

The Active approvals panel appears when any token account has a delegate. Each row shows the token, who is approved, whether that's an App / program or a Personal wallet (a red flag), and the amount allowed.

  1. Tick the approvals to remove (or use the top checkbox to select all).
  2. Click Revoke selected and approve.

Unwrap SOL

If you hold wrapped SOL, click Unwrap in its row to turn it back into normal SOL.

Row actions

Every token row has Chart (DexScreener) and Swap (opens the Swap tab with that token selected).

Swap

Trade tokens at the best available price across Solana exchanges, routed by Jupiter.

  1. Open Swap and connect your wallet.
  2. You pay: choose SOL or any token you hold. Enter an amount, or press Max (keeps 0.01 SOL for fees when paying with SOL).
  3. You receive: pick USDC, SOL, USDT, JUP or BONK — or choose Other token and paste a mint address.
  4. Slippage: 0.5% for stablecoins and large tokens; 1–3% normally; up to 10% for fast-moving memecoins.
  5. Click Get quote. You'll see the estimated amount, the minimum after slippage, price impact, the route, and a token check.
  6. Click Swap within about 30 seconds and approve in your wallet.
  • If the token you're buying is rated High risk or Likely scam, you must tick a box confirming you understand before swapping.
  • Price impact above 3% shows a warning — consider a smaller amount.
  • If a quote expires or a swap fails, press Get quote again.

Multi-send

Send SOL or any token to many wallets in a few transactions — airdrops, payroll, giveaways.

  1. Open Multi-send and connect.
  2. Pick the token to send.
  3. Paste recipients, one per line. Commas, spaces, tabs or semicolons all work: 
    RecipientWalletAddress1, 100
RecipientWalletAddress2 250
RecipientWalletAddress3
    (Replace these with real wallet addresses — the last line uses the default amount.) Lines without an amount use the Default amount box.
  4. Click Check list. OnTrace validates every address and amount, totals everything, flags duplicates, and checks your balance.
  5. Click Send, confirm, and approve each transaction (18 SOL transfers or 5 token transfers per transaction).
Costs: recipients who've never held the token need a token account created — about 0.002 SOL each, paid by you.
Transfers are final. If a batch stops midway, earlier transactions may already have gone through — check your wallet history before retrying.

Create token

Launch a Solana token with its name and symbol stored on-chain (Token-2022 standard).

Fields

FieldNotes
NameUp to 32 characters, e.g. OnTrace
SymbolUp to 10 characters, e.g. ONTRACE
Total supplyAll of it is minted to your wallet
Decimals6 is standard for most tokens; 9 matches SOL; 0 for whole-unit tokens
Metadata linkOptional link to a JSON file with your logo and description (below)
Lock supplyOn by default. Revokes mint authority so no more can ever be created — buyers trust this
Lock name & logoOff by default. Removes the ability to change name, symbol or metadata link forever

Freeze authority is never set, so nobody — including you — can freeze holders' tokens.

Metadata file

Host a JSON file (for example on your own website) and paste its link:

{
  "name": "My Token",
  "symbol": "MYTKN",
  "description": "What my token is about.",
  "image": "https://example.com/logo.png",
  "external_url": "https://example.com"
}

Steps

  1. Fill in the fields. The estimated cost (account deposits + network fee) appears beside the button.
  2. Click Create token, review the summary, and approve in your wallet.
  3. Copy the mint address shown afterwards — that's your token's ID.

Making it tradeable

A new token can't be bought or sold until it has a liquidity pool. Create one by pairing it with SOL on Raydium or Meteora. DexScreener lists it automatically once trading starts.

Your responsibility: creating and promoting tokens may be regulated where you live. Check local rules before launching publicly.

Token checker

  1. Open Token checker and paste a token's mint address (from DexScreener, Solscan or the project's site).
  2. Press Check token.

You'll get the risk rating with reasons, plus price, market cap, liquidity, 24h change, 24h trades, pair age, and whether mint and freeze authority are still active. Links take you to the chart or straight to Swap.

Trending

Solana tokens currently getting attention on DexScreener, refreshed when you open the tab or press Refresh.

  • Sort by 24h volume, 24h change, market cap or newest.
  • Boosted means the team paid for promotion — it isn't proof of real demand.
  • Each row shows a quick rating with its main reason, plus Chart, Check and Swap.

Watchlist & alerts

Watchlist

  1. Open Watchlist, paste a wallet address, add a label (e.g. Whale #1), and press Add.
  2. Each row shows SOL balance, portfolio value, token count and last activity.
  3. Activity shows the last 10 transactions explained in plain English — e.g. "Bought 12.5K BONK for 0.3 SOL". Graph opens the wallet in Wallet graph.

You can also add wallets from the Dev tracker and Wallet graph using Watch links. Your watchlist is saved only in this browser.

Live alerts

  1. Choose what to be alerted about: All activity, Buys, sells & swaps only, or Only moves above the SOL threshold.
  2. Press Start alerts and allow browser notifications when asked.
  3. OnTrace checks watched wallets every 30 seconds. New moves appear in the feed and as notifications. Anything flagged risky always alerts.
Keep the tab open. Alerts run while OnTrace is open in your browser. Closing the tab stops them.

Dev tracker

Investigate who's behind a token.

  1. Open Dev tracker and paste the token's mint address. If you already know the developer's wallet, paste it in the second box.
  2. Press Trace dev. OnTrace reads the token's history back to its very first transaction to find who created it.

You'll see:

  • Signals — warnings like "Dev still holds 15% of supply", "Dev sold this token 4 times recently", or "Top 10 wallets hold 62%".
  • Dev wallet — address, how it was identified, how much of the supply they hold, their SOL balance.
  • Dev's recent moves with this token, in plain English.
  • Top holders — the 20 biggest holders with % of supply. Pools/programs are labelled; the dev is marked DEV.

Very active tokens (over 15,000 transactions) may be too busy to trace back to the start — paste the dev wallet manually if you know it.

Wallet graph

  1. Open Wallet graph, paste an address, choose how many recent transactions to read (50, 100 or 200), and press Map wallet.
  2. The wallet sits in the centre. Around it are the wallets it interacts with most.
  • Line thickness — how often they interact.
  • Magenta — this wallet sent them SOL. Lime — they sent this wallet SOL.
  • Grey nodes — programs or pools, not people.
  • Click any node to explore that wallet next. The table below lists every counterparty with transaction counts, SOL in/out and tokens involved.

Useful for spotting a developer's linked wallets, insider clusters, or where funds went.

Transaction inspector

Explain a transaction

  1. Copy a transaction signature (from your wallet history, Solscan or anywhere).
  2. Paste it into Tx inspector and press Inspect.
  3. Read the plain-English summary, fee, programs used, warnings, and the full list of balance changes. Optionally enter a wallet to see it from that wallet's point of view.

Simulate before signing

Some sites can give you an unsigned transaction (a long base64 string). Paste it into the inspector to see exactly what it would do — before anything is signed.

WarningWhat it means
🚨 Hands ownership of your token accountThe classic Solana drainer trick: someone takes control of your tokens. The headline will say Do not sign.
⚠️ Approves someone to spend your tokensCreates an approval — check who and how much.
⚠️ Closes an account to someone elseThe account's SOL deposit goes to another address.
Calls unrecognised programsNot necessarily bad — just make sure you trust the site.

Note: the simulator can't yet decode unsigned transactions in Solana's newest format. You can still inspect those by signature after they're sent.

Every token gets one of four ratings. OnTrace combines live market data (DexScreener) with the token's on-chain settings, and the most serious signal wins.

Health score

Starts at 100 and loses points for:

IssuePointsMaximum
Each empty token account−2−20
Each Likely scam token−8−40
Each High risk token−4−20
Each active approval−5−15
Each dust token (worth under $0.50)−1−10

80+ is healthy, 50–79 needs attention, under 50 needs a clean-up. Reclaiming, burning and revoking raise your score.

Safety guide

🛡️ Built-in transaction guard. Every transaction OnTrace builds is checked and simulated before your wallet opens. It's blocked automatically if it would hand ownership of your token accounts to someone else, add a spending approval, move tokens you didn't ask to move, deliver less than your swap quote, spend more SOL than expected, call an unexpected program, or fail on-chain. You'll see "Running safety check…" then "Safety check passed" before your wallet asks you to approve. Details: Security.
  • Seed phrases are never needed. OnTrace — and any honest site — will never ask for yours.
  • Check the address bar: the only official site is ontrace.space. Bookmark it.
  • Read what your wallet shows before approving anything.
  • Ignore airdropped tokens that point to "claim" websites — they're usually drainers. Burn them instead.
  • Check before you buy with the Token checker; look for locked mint and freeze authority.
  • Revoke approvals you don't recognise.
  • Use a separate wallet for trying new apps and tokens.
  • Official accounts only: @0ntrace on X and team@ontrace.space. OnTrace will never DM you first.

Troubleshooting

ProblemFix
Button says Loading… for a long timeYour connection is slow or blocking the app's libraries. Refresh, or try another network/browser.
"No Solana wallet found"Install Phantom, Solflare or Backpack, then reload. On a phone, use Open in Phantom/Solflare.
Cleanup buttons are greyed outConnect the wallet you scanned, clear the address box, and press Scan. Read-only scans can't clean up.
"Connection cancelled or failed"You closed or rejected the wallet popup. Try again and approve.
"RPC may be blocking or rate-limiting you"Too many requests at once. Wait a minute and retry, or add your own RPC URL in Settings.
Swap quote fails / "No route found"The token may have no liquidity or isn't tradeable yet. Check it in Token checker.
Swap fails after approvingThe price moved. Get a new quote, raise slippage slightly, or raise the priority fee.
Transaction times outSolana is busy. Raise the priority fee in Settings and try again. Check your wallet history first to avoid repeating a transaction that went through.
Alerts stoppedAlerts only run while the tab is open. Re-open OnTrace and press Start alerts.
Dev tracker can't find the creatorThe token has too much history. Paste the dev wallet manually if known.
Swaps don't work on devnetExpected — swaps are mainnet only.
"OnTrace's safety check blocked this transaction"The guard found something wrong (the reason is shown). Nothing was signed or sent. If it says the transaction "would fail on-chain", check your balance or try again; for anything else, don't retry — report it to team@ontrace.space.
Custom RPC doesn't workFor security, only known providers are allowed: Helius, QuickNode, Alchemy, Triton (rpcpool), Ankr, PublicNode and Solana's own endpoints.

Glossary

Airdrop
Tokens sent to your wallet without you asking. Often spam or bait for scams.
Base64 transaction
An unsigned transaction written as text, which the inspector can simulate.
Boost
Paid promotion on DexScreener.
Burn
Permanently destroy tokens.
Drainer
A malicious transaction or site designed to take your assets once you sign.
Dust
Tiny token balances worth almost nothing.
Jupiter
Solana's main swap aggregator, which finds the best price across exchanges.
Transaction guard
OnTrace's automatic pre-signing check that simulates each transaction and blocks dangerous ones.
Lamport
The smallest unit of SOL (1 SOL = 1,000,000,000 lamports).
Mint address
A token's unique ID on Solana.
Rent
The refundable SOL deposit every account must hold.
RPC
The service apps use to read from and send to the Solana network.
Rug pull
When a token's creators drain its liquidity or dump their supply, crashing the price.
Signature
A transaction's unique ID, used to look it up.
Simulation
Running a transaction against current blockchain state without sending it, to preview the result.

For developers

OnTrace is a single static page — HTML, CSS and a JavaScript module, with no build step and no backend. The full source is on GitHub.

ComponentUsed for
@solana/web3.js 1.98 (self-hosted bundle)Connections, transactions, simulation
@solana/spl-token 0.4.9Token instructions and account decoding
@solana/spl-token-metadata 0.1.6On-chain token metadata
Solana JSON-RPC (direct)Transaction history, including version-1 transactions
Jupiter Swap APISwap quotes and transactions
DexScreener APIPrices, liquidity, trending

Security model: all libraries are bundled into vendor/solana.bundle.js and served from ontrace.space; a strict Content Security Policy allows scripts only from the site itself and connections only to known RPC providers, Jupiter and DexScreener. Every transaction passes guardTransaction() in app.js before the wallet is asked to sign.

Run locally: clone the repo and serve the folder over HTTP (e.g. npx serve .) — wallets don't connect to file:// pages. Configuration (RPC, batch sizes, trusted tokens) is at the top of the script in index.html.
